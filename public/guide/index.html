The 6 parts of every AI video pipeline

How short AI videos get made, from the first idea to the posted reel. Every part comes with real numbers from the 28 videos I made in 10 days, including the ones that flopped.

Before you start

Same six parts, any tools

Every AI video system has the same six parts, whatever tools it uses. I spent most of my time on part 4, the pictures. My data says the views are won or lost in part 1. I learned that the slow way.

Team
One person, a Mac and Claude Code

Part 1

Script

Decides what people hear and see, second by second.

Your options

Any chat AI (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini) can draft a script. The draft is easy. Judging it is the hard part.

What I use

Claude writes the drafts. A small checker script then reads each draft and warns me when it breaks one of my rules:

  • The first second shows something concrete. A face, an object, a number.
  • Something new happens right after the hook. No backstory between second 3 and second 10.
  • One story thread. Every number is said out loud before it appears on screen.
  • Sentences of 14 words or fewer, in everyday words.

The mistake I made

My early videos opened with a strong hook and then explained the background. People left at second 10. Across my tracked videos, only about half the viewers were still watching at 10 seconds (48–57 %).

When I fixed it

My newest video follows all four rules. Line 2 is a new event. The video stays in one place until the last line. 71 % were still watching at second 10. That's one video, and it changed more than one thing. So treat it as a hint.

Try this

Write your hook, then write what happens at second 4. If second 4 is an explanation, cut it and show the next event instead.

Part 2

Voice

Turns the script into speech.

Your options

Text-to-speech services such as ElevenLabs, or your own voice recorded on a phone.

What I use

An ElevenLabs voice, which I run through Higgsfield. A new voice track for a whole video costs a few credits.

The mistake I made

I sped up the hook to make it punchier. You could hear the tempo jump. Now every line runs at the same speed.

Try this

Check the voice service's terms before you use it for paid work. Free plans often don't allow commercial use. And think about using your own voice. I think a real voice earns more trust, and it costs nothing.

Part 3

Timing

Finds the exact moment each word is spoken. Captions, cuts and sound effects all hang on these timestamps.

Your options

Speech-to-text tools give you word timestamps. Whisper is free and open source. Some voice services return timestamps directly.

What I use

Word timestamps from the voice file. A script turns them into caption screens and a cut list.

The mistake I made

Captions came up to 0.65 seconds after the word. It felt off, even before I could say why. Now no caption comes more than 0.1 seconds after its word.

Try this

Build captions from timestamps, never by hand. You will change the script often, and the captions should follow by themselves.

Part 4

Pictures

Everything the viewer sees.

Your options

There are three main routes, and each one costs you something different.

Stock footage and templatesLowNo character of your ownEasy

What I use

Code. The mascot, the sets and the infographics are 3D scenes written in Swift (SceneKit) by Claude Code. A test frame in my 3D set renders in about 0.06 seconds.

The mistake I made

I built one video in four versions. Code makes changes cheap, which makes it easy to keep polishing instead of posting.

Try this

Pick one route for your first ten videos. Mixing all three eats your time.

Part 5

Sound

Music, sound effects and the final mix.

Your options

Music libraries with a licence for social media, or music you make yourself.

What I use

The music and every sound effect are generated in code. Each visual event gets its own sound. A splash gets a splash, a stamp gets a thud.

The mistake I made

I left a 0.6-second silent pause in the middle of one video. Voice, music and room sound all stopped. Watching it back, it felt like the video was over. On a feed, a video that seems over gets swiped away. Pauses now last half a second at most, and the background sound keeps going.

Try this

Watch your video once with your eyes closed. Every moment without sound is a moment someone can leave.

Part 6

Checks and posting

Catches mistakes before the world sees them, then tells you what worked.

Your options

Your own eyes, or small scripts that check the same things every time.

What I check

  • Loudness: I mix to −14 LUFS, a common target for social and streaming platforms.
  • Captions: they stay inside a safe box where the app's buttons don't cover them.
  • Reading time: every caption stays up long enough to read. Netflix's subtitle guide allows about 20 characters per second for adults, so I stay under that.
  • Overlap: captions never cover an infographic.

How I read the numbers

I wait until a video has more than 1,000 viewers before judging it. Below that, the numbers jump around too much. I change one thing per video, so I know what made the difference.

The mistake I made

My videos looked good and still stopped at about 1,200 viewers. The four I have full stats for reached 1,229, 1,324, 1,207 and 1,131 viewers. The pictures were fine. The stories lost people at second 10.

What the newest video showed

It kept 71 % at second 10. It still stopped at 1,069 viewers, with 0 shares. Holding people longer didn't bring more reach on its own. Shares are what I'm working on next.
